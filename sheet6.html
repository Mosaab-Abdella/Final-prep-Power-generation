Formula sheet (Lecture 9)

Two cycles in series: η = η₁ + η₂ − η₁η₂ (no heat lost between them)
With a loss QL between the plants (xL = QL/Q₁): η = η₁ + η₂(1 − η₁ − xL)
Parallel plants: η lies between η₁ and η₂ (no benefit); series-parallel with supplementary firing: η is less than that of two cycles in series
Binary mercury–steam: mHg (hHg,exit − hHg,cond) = ms (hsteam,sat/sup − hfeed) (≈ 7–8 kg of mercury per kg of steam)
Heat-recovery boiler: ṁg·cp,g·(Tin − Tstack) = Σ ṁsteam(hout − hfeed)
Cogeneration: energy utilisation = (net work + process heat)/fuel energy; back-pressure turbine: the exhaust is the process steam
  • Dual-pressure steam raises more heat from the gas (lower stack temperature) because the gas–steam temperature difference is smaller (Lecture 9, pinch points).
  • Steam from IF97 tables: read superheated values by (p, T), saturated by p or T; for a stated turbine efficiency use h₂ = h₁ − η(h₁ − h₂ₛ).

Gas turbine + steam bottoming cycle (Sheet 6 Q2)

Same structure as Fall 2023 Q4No sheet solution

Gas turbine, ηc = ηt = 0.85, r = 5, inlet 20 °C, turbine inlet 670 °C, output 8 MW (air as working fluid). Exhaust passes through a boiler producing steam at 16 bar, 240 °C from 40 °C feed water; boiler efficiency 0.72 reckoned on a final gas temperature of 20 °C. The steam expands in a turbine (η = 0.75) to 0.08 bar. Find the additional power and the overall thermal efficiency.

  1. T₂ = 494.5 K; T₄ = 647.6 K; wnet = 94.6 kJ/kg; ṁair = 8000/94.6 = 84.5 kg/s
  2. Heat added in the combustor = ṁcp(T₃ − T₂) = 38,118 kW
  3. Boiler. Heat available above 20 °C = ṁcp(T₄ − 293.15) = 30,118 kW; steam receives 0.72× = 21,685 kW.
  4. Steam properties. 16 bar, 240 °C (superheated table): h₁ = 2895.6; feed water 40 °C: hf = 167.5. Turbine: h₂ = h₁ − 0.75(h₁ − h₂ₛ) = 2278.7 kJ/kg (x = 0.876).
  5. ṁs = 21,685/(2895.6 − 167.5) = 7.95 kg/s; additional power = ṁs(h₁ − h₂) = 4,903 kW
  6. ηoverall = (8000 + 4,903)/38,118 = 33.9 % (gas turbine alone 21.0 %)

Final answer

  • Additional power ≈ 4.90 MW (steam 7.95 kg/s)
  • ηoverall ≈ 33.9 %
Pump workPump work for 16 bar is about 1.6 kJ/kg and is neglected; include ṁsv∆p if you want to be exact.

Dual-pressure heat-recovery steam generator (Sheet 6 Q4)

Lecture 9No sheet solution

Dual-pressure steam generator in a combined cycle: gases from supplementary firing at 650 °C (200 % theoretical air, stoichiometric A/F = 15) leave at 176 °C; steam at 68 bar, 537 °C and 13.6 bar, 260 °C from feed water at 148 °C; equal HP and LP steam flows. Find (a) the mass of gas per kg of total steam, (b) the gas exhaust temperature if only HP steam is made for the same gas flow.

  1. Enthalpies. h(68 bar, 537 °C) = 3502.5; h(13.6 bar, 260 °C) = 2952.5; hf(148 °C) = 623.6 kJ/kg.
  2. Heat per kg of HP steam = 2878.8; per kg of LP steam = 2328.9. For 1 kg of total steam (½ kg of each):
    q = ½(2878.8 + 2328.9) = 2603.9 kJ
  3. (a) Gas: cp = 1.15 kJ/kg·K (assumed; not given)
    mg = q/(cp(650 − 176)) = 2603.9/(1.15×474) = 4.78 kg gas per kg steam
  4. (b) Same gas flow, HP steam only (the same ½ kg of HP steam per kg of total steam of case (a)): the gas gives up only ½×2878.8 kJ per 4.78 kg:
    Texh = 650 − (0.5×2878.8)/(1.15×4.78) = 388 °C

Final answer

  • (a) 4.78 kg gas per kg steam
  • (b) exhaust ≈ 388 °C (much hotter: heat is wasted)
State your assumptionscp,gas and the reading of (b) are not given. I took cp = 1.15 and kept the HP-steam flow unchanged. If instead the HP flow is raised until the stack is 176 °C again, ṁHP = ṁgcp(474)/ΔhHP = 0.90 kg per kg of total steam of case (a).

Diesel plant exhaust-heat distillation (Sheet 6 Q6)

Links Sheet 4 and Lecture 5No sheet solution

A 3000 BHP diesel plant burns 0.19 kg fuel per BHP·h; air/fuel = 22; exhaust leaves the engine at 450 °C; jacket water 20 → 80 °C. The hot jacket water goes to an evaporator that makes dry saturated steam at 1.05 bar, heated by the exhaust gases (leave at 140 °C, cp = 1.088); the steam is condensed to distilled water. Neglect blow-down and losses. Find the distilled water per hour.

  1. Fuel = 0.19×3000 = 570 kg/h = 0.1583 kg/s; exhaust gas = (22 + 1)×0.1583 = 3.642 kg/s.
  2. Heat from the exhaust = 3.642×1.088×(450 − 140) = 1,228 kW
  3. Steam tables: dry saturated steam at 1.05 bar: hg = 2677.1 kJ/kg (Tsat = 101.0 °C). Feed = jacket water at 80 °C: hf(80 °C) = 334.9 kJ/kg.
  4. ṁdistillate = 1,228/(2677.1 − 334.9) = 0.524 kg/s = 1,888 kg/h

Final answer

  • ≈ 1,888 kg/h of distilled water

Back-pressure turbine for process steam (Sheet 6 Q7)

CogenerationNo sheet solution

A textile factory needs 18 t/h of dry saturated steam at 3 bar and 1 MW of power. A back-pressure turbo-generator supplies both: its exhaust is the process steam. Turbine internal efficiency 0.65; combined mechanical and generator efficiency 0.86. Find the initial state of the steam.

  1. ṁ = 18 000/3600 = 5.0 kg/s. Turbine shaft power = 1000/0.86 = 1162.8 kW ⇒ actual work = 232.6 kJ/kg.
  2. Exhaust = dry saturated at 3 bar: h₂ = hg(3 bar) = 2724.9. So h₁ = h₂ + 232.6 = 2957.4 kJ/kg.
  3. Isentropic drop = 232.6/0.65 = 357.8 ⇒ h₂ₛ = 2957.4 − 357.8 = 2599.7. At 3 bar, hf = 561.5 and hg = 2724.9 ⇒ x₂ₛ = (2599.7 − 561.5)/(2724.9 − 561.5) = 0.942, so s₁ = sf + x₂ₛsfg = 6.684 kJ/kg·K.
  4. State 1 is the point with h = 2957.4 and s = 6.684: on the Mollier chart go to those two values; from the superheated tables you find p₁ ≈ 18.5 bar, T₁ ≈ 270 °C.

Final answer

  • Initial steam ≈ 18.5 bar, 270 °C (h₁ = 2957 kJ/kg)
Mollier useThe two numbers (h₁ and s₁) fix the state, so you can read p₁ and T₁ directly from the Mollier chart: intersect the horizontal line h = h₁ with the vertical line s = s₁.
