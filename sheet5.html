Formula sheet (gas turbine plants)

Isentropic: T₂ₛ = T₁·r(γ−1)/γ (air: γ = 1.4, exponent 0.2857; gas: γ = 1.333, exponent 0.25)
Compressor: ηc = (T₂ₛ − T₁)/(T₂ − T₁), wc = cpa(T₂ − T₁) Turbine: ηt = (T₃ − T₄)/(T₃ − T₄ₛ), wt = cpg(T₃ − T₄)
wnet = wt − wc; work ratio = wnet/wt; ηth = wnet/qin, qin = cp(T₃ − T₂)
Combustor: ṁf·CV = ṁgcpT₃ − ṁacpT₂ with ṁg = ṁa(1 + 1/AF) (when the fuel mass is not negligible)
Intercooling (ideal, back to T₁): equal stage ratios rs = √r, work saved; Reheating: expand to an intermediate p, reheat to T₃, expand again; Regeneration: effectiveness ε = (Tair out − Tair in)/(Texhaust − Tair in)
Compressor driven by the HP turbine: wHPT·ηmech = wc ⇒ the HP turbine temperature drop is fixed; the power turbine takes the rest
Pressure ratio for maximum work: ropt = (T₃/T₁)γ/(2(γ−1)) (ideal cycle, constant cp)
  • Intercooling and reheating increase work but lower efficiency unless regeneration is added; regeneration helps at low pressure ratios (Lecture 7).
  • Use cp = 1.005, γ = 1.4 before the combustor and cp = 1.15, γ = 1.333 after it when both are given; otherwise one cp and γ for the whole cycle.

Simple open cycle with losses

Lecture 8 exampleNo sheet solution: derived here

Open-cycle gas turbine: air at 1 bar, 20 °C; compressor ratio 4; ηc = 0.80, ηt = 0.85; air/fuel = 90; air flow 3 kg/s. Find the power developed and the thermal efficiency. (Lecture 8 example: cp = 1.0 kJ/kg·K, γ = 1.4, CV = 41 800 kJ/kg.)

  1. T₂ₛ = 293.15×40.2857 = 435.6 K; T₂ = 293.15 + (435.6 − 293.15)/0.8 = 471.2 K
  2. Combustion: heat from fuel per kg air = CV/AF = 41 800/90 = 464.4 kJ. Gas mass = 1 + 1/90 = 1.0111 kg:
    464.4 = 1.0111×1.0×T₃ − 1.0×471.2 ⇒ T₃ = 925.4 K
  3. T₄ₛ = T₃/40.2857 = 622.7 K; T₄ = T₃ − 0.85(T₃ − T₄ₛ) = 668.1 K
  4. wt = 1.0111×1.0×(T₃ − T₄) = 260.1; wc = 178.1; wnet = 82.0 kJ/kg air
  5. P = 3×82.0 = 246 kW; η = wnet/(heat from fuel) = 82.0/464.4 = 17.7 %

Final answer

  • P ≈ 246 kW, η ≈ 17.7 %

Gas turbine with a waste-heat boiler

Exam-styleNo sheet solution: derived here

Gas turbine (ηc = ηt = 0.85, r = 6, 25 °C inlet, 700 °C turbine inlet, 10 MW) with a waste-heat boiler making dry saturated steam at 6 bar from 30 °C feed water; the water absorbs 97 % of the heat removed from the gas; gas leaves at 180 °C. CV = 43 100 kJ/kg, combustion efficiency 0.98; air as the working fluid. Find the steam rate, fuel rate and overall efficiency.

  1. T₂ = 532.6 K, T₄ = 641.7 K; wt = 333.1, wc = 235.7, wnet = 97.4 kJ/kg
  2. ṁair = 10 000/97.4 = 102.7 kg/s
  3. Fuel. Combustor heat = ṁcp(T₃ − T₂) = 45,446 kW ⇒ ṁf = 45,446/(0.98×43 100) = 1.076 kg/s = 3,873 kg/h.
  4. Waste-heat boiler. Heat removed from the gas = ṁcp(T₄ − 453.15) = 19,455 kW; steam gets 0.97× = 18,872 kW. Steam tables: hg(6 bar) = 2756.1 (Tsat = 158.8 °C), hf(30 °C) = 125.7.
    ṁs = 18,872/(2756.1 − 125.7) = 7.17 kg/s = 25,828 kg/h
  5. Efficiency. Fuel energy = 46,374 kW. Power only: 21.6 %. Overall (power + steam heat):
    ηoverall = (10 000 + 18,872)/46,374 = 62.3 %

Final answer

  • Steam ≈ 25,828 kg/h; fuel ≈ 3,873 kg/h
  • ηoverall ≈ 62 % (power-only efficiency 21.6 %)
Define “overall”The sheet says “overall efficiency of the plant”. With a heat-recovery boiler that normally means (net work + useful steam heat)/fuel energy. State this definition and also give the power-only value.

Intercooled, reheated, regenerative plant (the lecture template)

Lecture 7–8 templateNo sheet solution: derived here

5000 kW plant, overall pressure ratio 9 in two equal compressor stages with ideal intercooling (inlet 27 °C); the HP turbine drives both compressors; gases enter it at 750 °C and are reheated to 750 °C before the LP (power) turbine. The LP exhaust heats the HP-compressor air in a heat exchanger (thermal ratio 0.75). ηc = 0.85, ηt = 0.87, ηmech = 0.95 on both shafts. Air: cp = 1.005, γ = 1.4; gases: cp = 1.15, γ = 1.333. Fuel mass neglected. Find the air mass flow and thermal efficiency.

  1. Compressors (each r = √9 = 3):
    T₂ₛ = 300.15×30.2857 = 410.8 K; ΔT per stage = (410.8 − 300.15)/0.85 = 130.2 K
    wc = 2×1.005×130.2 = 261.7 kJ/kg
  2. HP turbine only drives the compressors:
    wHPT×0.95 = wc ⇒ wHPT = 275.5 kJ/kg ⇒ ΔT = 275.5/1.15 = 239.6 K ⇒ T₇ = 1023.15 − 239.6 = 783.6 K
  3. HP turbine pressure ratio from ηt = ΔT/[T₆(1 − r−0.25)]:
    1 − r−0.25 = 239.6/(0.87×1023.15) ⇒ rHPT = 3.508, p₇ = 9/3.508 = 2.566 bar
  4. LP turbine (p₈ = 2.566 bar → 1 bar, r = 2.566):
    T₉ₛ = 1023.15/2.5660.25 = 808.6 K; T₉ = 1023.15 − 0.87(1023.15 − 808.6) = 836.5 K
    wLPT = 1.15×(1023.15 − 836.5) = 214.7; net to the generator = 0.95×214.7 = 204.0 kJ/kg
  5. Mass flow: ṁ = 5000/204.0 = 24.51 kg/s.
  6. Regenerator. Air leaves the HP compressor at T₄ = 430.4 K:
    T₅ = T₄ + 0.75(T₉ − T₄) = 430.4 + 0.75(836.5 − 430.4) = 734.9 K
  7. Heat supplied (main combustor + reheater):
    q = 1.15(1023.15 − 734.9) + 1.15(1023.15 − 783.6) = 606.9 kJ/kg
    η = 204.0/606.9 = 33.6 %

Final answer

  • ṁair ≈ 24.5 kg/s
  • ηth ≈ 33.6 %
  • work ratio ≈ 0.44

4

Combined plant with supplementary firing (part (a) only)

Long multi-partOnly part (a) solved

Combined-cycle plant: four 60 MWe gas turbines and one 150 MWe steam turbine; fuel A/Fstoich = 16, actual 400 % theoretical air; GT inlet 15 °C, 1 bar, TIT 1040 °C, pressure ratio 5, ηisentropic = 0.87 and ηmech = 0.92 for compressor and turbine; supplementary firing to 840 °C (200 % theoretical air); steam 80 bar, 540 °C with a deaerator raising the feed water to 240 °C; condenser 0.07 bar; all generator efficiencies 0.98. Find (a) air flow per gas turbine, (b) steam flow from the heat-recovery boiler, (c) heat added and stack temperature, (d) cycle efficiencies.

  1. Actual A/F = 4×16 = 64 (400 % of theoretical air), so f = 1/64 fuel per kg air.
  2. T₂ = 288.15 + 288.15(50.2857 − 1)/0.87 = 481.5 K; wc = 1.005×(481.5 − 288.15) = 194.3 kJ/kg air
  3. T₄ = 1313.15 − 0.87×1313.15(1 − 5−0.25) = 934.9 K = 662 °C; wt = (1 + 1/64)×1.15×(1313.15 − 934.9) = 441.7 kJ/kg air
  4. Electrical output per kg air = ηgen(ηmechwt − wc/ηmech) = 0.98×(0.92×441.7 − 194.3/0.92) = 191.3 kJ/kg.
  5. (a) ṁair = 60 000/191.3 = 314 kg/s per gas turbine

Final answer

  • (a) ṁair ≈ 314 kg/s per turbine (T₄ = 662 °C)
Parts (b)–(d) need data the sheet does not giveThe steam-turbine efficiency and the fuel calorific value are not stated, so the steam flow, heat added and efficiencies cannot be fixed uniquely. The method: (1) heat added in supplementary firing = ṁgcp(840 − T₄) with the extra fuel for 400 % → 200 % air; (2) HRSG balance gas 840 °C → stack = steam flow × (h(80 bar, 540 °C) − hf(240 °C)) for the economizer–boiler–superheater; (3) steam flow from 150 MWe/(ηgenwST) with the state of the bled steam; (4) η = P/Q for each cycle and for the combination. Ask your instructor which ηST and CV to use.
