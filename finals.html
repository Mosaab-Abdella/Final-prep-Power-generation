Fall 2026 and 2025 · Q2 (16 pts): open heater, bled at 3 bar

Also Fall 2024 (4 bar)Every final since 2024

Steam at 20 bar, 350 °C is supplied to a turbine and bled at 3 bar. The bled steam just comes out saturated and heats water in an open heater to its saturation state. The rest expands to 0.1 bar. The turbine efficiency is the same before and after bleeding. Requirements: A flow diagram, B h–s diagram, C h and quality at exit of the last stage, D bled mass per kg, E pumping power per kg, F net power per kg, G overall cycle efficiency.

Parts A and B are marks you can bank

A: flow diagram with numbered points (boiler, turbine with one bleed, condenser, pump 1, OFWH, pump 2). B: h–s with the bled point on the saturated-vapour line, the dashed isentropic drops, and the 20 / 3 / 0.1 bar lines. Practise drawing both in under 3 minutes.

  • C: h₃ ≈ 2231 kJ/kg, x₃ ≈ 0.852
  • D: y ≈ 0.146 kg per kg
  • E: pumping power ≈ 2.07 kW per kg/s
  • F: net power ≈ 833 kW per kg/s
  • G: η ≈ 32.3 %
Using the 4 bar version (Fall 2024) — read this

At 20 bar/350 °C the entropy is s₁ = 6.9582, which is larger than sg at 4 bar (6.8954). An isentropic expansion to 4 bar is already superheated, so a real (irreversible) expansion cannot end up saturated at 4 bar: it would need η > 1 (the numbers give η = 1.07). That is why the later papers (2025, 2026) were changed to 3 bar. If you meet the 4 bar version, state this in one line and use 3 bar, or use the 300 °C version solved on the Sheet 2 page (Q6).

Marks lost on this question

Neglecting the pumps (parts E and F ask for them). Applying (1 − y) to pump 2: the feed pump handles the whole flow. Taking h₅ = h₄ in the heater balance when the question wants pump power.

Fall 2023 · Q1 (10 pts): load curve, duration curve, units

Lecture 1–2

Loads: 0–6 h 30 MW, 6–12 h 90, 12–14 h 60, 14–18 h 100, 18–24 h 50. Supply with three 30 MW units and one 10 MW unit, plus a standby equal to the largest (30 MW). a) load curve, b) load-duration curve, c) load factor, d) capacity of the plant and capacity factor, e) plant use factor.

Check the data on your paper

I read the table from a photo as: 0–6 h 30 MW, 6–12 h 90, 12–14 h 60, 14–18 h 100, 18–24 h 50 (handwritten durations 6, 6, 2, 4, 6). If your copy differs, only the numbers change.

  1. a) Load curve: step plot above.
  2. b) Load-duration curve: sort loads high to low, accumulate the hours (100 MW 4 h, 90 MW 6 h, 60 MW 2 h, 50 MW 6 h, 30 MW 6 h).
  3. c) Load factor.
    E = 30×6 + 90×6 + 60×2 + 100×4 + 50×6 = 1540 MWh avg = 64.17 MW LF = 64.17/100 = 0.642
  4. d) Capacity and capacity factor. Installed = 3×30 + 10 + 30 (standby) = 130 MW.
    CF = average/capacity = 64.17/130 = 0.494
    (If you count only the 100 MW of working units: 0.642.)
  5. e) Plant use factor = energy/(capacity × hours operated) = 1540/(130×24) = 0.494: the plant operates all 24 h, so it equals the capacity factor.

Final answer

  • c) LF ≈ 0.642
  • d) capacity 130 MW (100 MW + 30 MW standby), CF ≈ 0.494
  • e) use factor ≈ 0.494
Why the 130 MW?

The question states the standby unit is part of the plant (equal to the largest unit, 30 MW). State your capacity definition in one line, then both interpretations are defensible.

Fall 2023 · Q3 (10 pts): economizer + air preheater

Sheet 3 Q6 variant

Gas into economizer 700 °C, out of air preheater 150 °C; water 80 → 180 °C; air 25 → 200 °C; fuel 4 t/h; air/fuel 17; cp,gas = 1.15; U = 45 and 30 W/m²K; counter-flow. Find both areas and the steam generator capacity.

Same as Sheet 3 Q6, but now the gas exit temperature (150 °C) is given, so no assumption is needed. cp,gas = 1.15, cp,air = 1.0035, cp,w = 4.18.

  1. Flows: ṁf = 4/3.6 = 1.111 kg/s, ṁair = 18.89, ṁgas = 20.00 kg/s.
  2. Air preheater:
    Q = 18.89×1.0035×175 = 3,317 kW → Tg2 = 150 + 3,317/(20.0×1.15) = 294.2 °C
    LMTD = (94.2 − 125)/ln(94.2/125) → 108.9 °C A = 3,317,125/(30×108.9) = 1015 m²
  3. Economizer:
    Q = 20.0×1.15×(700 − 294.2) = 9,333 kW ṁw = Q/(4.18×100) = 22.3 kg/s
    ΔT₁ = 520, ΔT₂ = 214.2 → LMTD = 344.8 °C A = 9,332,875/(45×344.8) = 601 m²

  • Aair preheater ≈ 1015 m²

Fall 2023 · Q4 (12 pts): gas turbine + steam turbine combined cycle

Lectures 7–9Fall 2023 only so farNo official solution: derived here

Combined-cycle plant. Compressor inlet 1 bar, 25 °C; pressure ratio 8; maximum gas temperature 900 °C; ηcomp = ηGT = 88 %; fuel liquid octane (C₈H₁₈), CV 44 MJ/kg; cp,air = 1.005. Steam turbine inlet 40 bar, 425 °C; condenser 0.07 bar; feed water to the HRSG 170 °C; steam-turbine efficiency 85 %; steam flow 30 kg/s. a) T–s diagram, b) air-to-fuel ratio, c) total power output, d) overall efficiency, e) the lost-heat coefficient xL.

For a two-cycle plant, draw the two T–s diagrams side by side and show the exhaust 4 → stack as the heat that feeds the HRSG.

(b) Air-to-fuel ratio (gas cycle, per kg of air)

  1. T₂ₛ = T₁·80.2857 = 298.15×1.8114 = 540.1 K; T₂ = T₁ + (T₂ₛ − T₁)/0.88 = 573.1 K
  2. q₁ = cp(T₃ − T₂) = 1.005×(1173.15 − 573.1) = 603.1 kJ/kg air
  3. Combustor balance (fuel mass neglected in the gas flow): ṁf·CV = ṁa·q₁ ⇒
    A/F = CV/q₁ = 44 000/603.1 = 73.0

(c) Power output

  1. T₄ₛ = T₃/80.2857 = 647.6 K; T₄ = T₃ − 0.88(T₃ − T₄ₛ) = 710.7 K = 437.5 °C
  2. wT = 464.8; wC = 276.3; wnet = 188.5 kJ/kg air; ηgas = wnet/q₁ = 31.3 %
  3. Steam properties (superheated table, 40 bar 425 °C): h₅ = 3273.0, s₅ = 6.8567. At 0.07 bar the isentropic exit is h₆ₛ = 2129.2; h₆ = h₅ − 0.85(h₅ − h₆ₛ) = 2300.8 kJ/kg (x = 0.887). Feed water at 170 °C: h₈ = hf(170 °C) = 719.2.
  4. HRSG balance. Heat to the steam = 30×(3273.0 − 719.2) = 76,615 kW. The gas is cooled from T₄ to the feed-water temperature (170 °C stack): ṁacp(T₄ − 443.15) = 76,615 ⇒
    ṁa = 76,615/(1.005×(710.7 − 443.15)) = 285 kg/s
  5. Pgas = ṁawnet = 53.7 MW; Psteam = 30×(3273.0 − 2300.8) = 29.2 MW; total = 82.9 MW

(d) and (e) Overall efficiency and xL

  1. Q₁ = ṁaq₁ = 171.8 MW (fuel 3.91 kg/s); ηoverall = 82.9/171.8 = 48.2 %
  2. Heat lost between the plants = heat in the exhaust above ambient − heat given to the steam = ṁacp(T₄ − T₁) − Qs = 41.5 MW:
    xL = QL/Q₁ = 41.5/171.8 = 0.242
  3. Check with the lecture formula η = η₁ + η₂(1 − η₁ − xL) = 0.3125 + 0.3807×(1 − 0.3125 − 0.242) = 0.4823 ✓ (η₂ = Psteam/Qs).

Final answer

  • (b) A/F ≈ 73
  • (c) Ptotal ≈ 82.9 MW (53.7 MW gas + 29.2 MW steam); ṁair ≈ 285 kg/s
  • (d) ηoverall ≈ 48.2 %
  • (e) xL ≈ 0.242
Assumption you must write downThe paper gives the steam flow (30 kg/s) and the feed-water temperature (170 °C) but no stack temperature or air flow. I fixed the air flow by letting the exhaust gas cool down to the feed-water temperature in the HRSG (stack = 170 °C). I also neglected pump work and the fuel mass in the gas flow. If your instructor's solution takes a different stack temperature, the air flow and total power change in proportion, while A/F (73) does not.

Fall 2024 · Q4 (11 pts): dry cooling tower with surface condenser

Lectures 10–11Lecture example 2

Stand-alone steam plant, dry cooling tower with surface condenser. Full-load output 15 MW at 38 % thermal efficiency. Condenser: cooling-water rise 12 °C, TTD 5 °C, U = 3500 W/m²K. Tower: air 20 °C, 50 % RH, approach 10 °C, air exit 37 °C, U = 350 W/m²K, correction factor 0.96, induced-draft fan η = 75 %, air-side pressure drop 5 cm water. Draw the combined condenser–tower temperature diagram, then find 1) condensing temperature and pressure, 2) condenser area, 3) tower area, 4) fan power.

  1. Condensing temperature. Stack the four temperature differences on the ambient air temperature:
    Tc = Ta,i + approach + CR + TTD = 20 + 10 + 12 + 5 = 47 °C
    pc = psat(47 °C) = 0.1063 bar (saturation table, entered by temperature).
  2. Heat to reject. Heat input = 15/0.38 = 39.47 MW ⇒ rejected = 39.47 − 15 = 24.47 MW (this is the 39.47 MW in your handwritten note).
  3. Condenser. Cooling water: 30 → 42 °C; steam at 47 °C. ΔT₁ = 17, ΔT₂ = 5:
    LMTD = (17 − 5)/ln(17/5) = 9.81 °C; Ac = Q/(U·LMTD) = 24,473,684/(3500×9.81) = 713 m²
  4. Tower (counter-flow: water 42 → 30 °C, air 20 → 37 °C): ΔT₁ = 42 − 37 = 5, ΔT₂ = 30 − 20 = 10:
    LMTD = (10 − 5)/ln(10/5) = 7.21 °C; AT = Q/(U·F·LMTD) = 24,473,684/(350×0.96×7.21) = 10,098 m²
  5. Fan power. Air humidity (50 % at 20 °C): pv = 0.5×psat(20 °C) = 1.170 kPa ⇒ ψ = 0.622pv/(p − pv) = 0.00726 kg/kg. Dry-air flow from the air-side energy balance: ṁda = Q/(cp,ma·ΔTa) = 24,473,684/(1018.7×17) = 1413 kg/s. An induced-draft fan sits on the hot side, so use the exit-air density ρ = 1.133 kg/m³: V̇ = ṁda(1 + ψ)/ρ = 1256 m³/s.
    Pfan = Δp·V̇/η = (0.05×1000×9.81 = 490.5 Pa)×1256/0.75 = 821 kW

Final answer

  • 1) Tc = 47 °C, pc = 0.106 bar
  • 2) Acondenser ≈ 713 m²
  • 3) Atower ≈ 10,098 m²
  • 4) Pfan ≈ 821 kW (≈ 776 kW with inlet-air density)
This is Example 2 of Lecture 10The lecture gives Tc = 47 °C and pc = 0.106 bar. If your instructor uses a rounder air density (1.2 kg/m³), the fan power comes out near 0.78 MW; state which density you used.
