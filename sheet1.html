Sheet 1 · Economic factors · Lectures 1–2

Sheet 1 guide

Five problems, all one idea: energy ÷ (some kW × some hours). The solutions come from the Nag textbook examples in your upload; here they are rebuilt step by step with the diagrams the exam can ask for.

Definitions to memorise

Load factor = average load / peak load Capacity factor = average load / installed capacity
Demand factor = max demand / connected load Diversity factor = Σ individual max demands / simultaneous max demand
Use factor = energy / (capacity × hours of operation) Reserve factor = LF / CF

One year = 8760 h. Annual energy = average load × 8760.

1

Load curve, load factor, standby unit

Lecture 1–2 definitionExam hit: Finals 2023 variant

A power station supplies: 0–6 h 30 MW; 6–10 h 70; 10–12 h 90; 12–16 h 60; 16–20 h 100; 20–22 h 80; 22–24 h 60. (a) Draw the load curve and find the load factor. (b) Load factor and use factor of a 30 MW standby unit that takes all load above 70 MW.

(a) Load factor

Energy = 30×6 + 70×4 + 90×2 + 60×4 + 100×4 + 80×2 + 60×2 = 1560 MWh
Average load = 1560/24 = 65 MW LF = average/peak = 65/100 = 0.65

(b) Standby unit of 30 MW taking everything above 70 MW

Strip the part of each bar above the 70 MW line: 20 MW for 2 h (10–12), 30 MW for 4 h (16–20), 10 MW for 2 h (20–22).

Estandby = 20×2 + 30×4 + 10×2 = 180 MWh running time = 2 + 4 + 2 = 8 h
Average load while running = 180/8 = 22.5 MW LF = 22.5/30 = 0.75
Use factor = energy/(capacity × hours of operation) = 180000/(30 000×8) = 0.75

Final answer

  • (a) LF = 0.65
  • (b) LFstandby = 0.75, use factor = 0.75
Why two answers coincide

The standby's load factor and use factor are equal here because both are measured only over the 8 h it runs. If you measured over 24 h you would get 0.25. The sheet (and the book) measure over its running hours, so write “over its operating hours”.

2

Average load, demand factor, diversity factor

Exam hit: Finals 2024 & 2025Textbook solution included

Peak load 60 MW; connected maximum demands 30, 20, 10, 14 MW; plant capacity 80 MW; annual load factor 0.50. Find (a) average load, (b) annual energy, (c) demand factor, (d) diversity factor.

Given

  • Peak load 60 MW
  • Connected maximum demands 30, 20, 10, 14 MW
  • Capacity 80 MW, annual LF = 0.5

Find

  • (a) average load, (b) annual energy, (c) demand factor, (d) diversity factor
  1. (a) LF = average/peak → average = 0.5×60 = 30 MW.
  2. (b) Energy = average × 8760 h = 30×8760 = 262 800 MWh = 262.8×10⁶ kWh.
  3. (c) Demand factor = maximum demand ÷ connected load = 60/(30+20+10+14) = 60/74 = 0.811.
  4. (d) Diversity factor = sum of individual maximum demands ÷ simultaneous maximum demand = 74/60 = 1.233.

Final answer

  • (a) 30 MW (b) 262.8×10⁶ kWh (c) DF = 0.811 (d) diversity factor = 1.233
Do not swap the two

The same 74 MW number sits in both. In the demand factor it is the denominator (< 1 result); in the diversity factor it is the numerator (> 1 result). The plant capacity (80 MW) is not used in any of the four answers.

Exam hit

This exact question is Question 1 of the Fall 2024 and Fall 2025 finals (and in Fall 2026 the boiler drum took its place). Get it perfect and fast.

3

Revenue and capacity factor

Textbook solution included

A 210 MW thermal plant, maximum load 160 MW, annual LF 0.6. Coal 1 kg/kWh at $6.34 per tonne. (a) Annual revenue if energy sells at 1.4 cent/kWh, (b) capacity factor.

  1. Average load. LF = 0.6 → 0.6×160 = 96 MW.
  2. Annual energy. 96×8760 = 840,960 MWh = 840,960,000 kWh.
  3. Income. 840,960,000 × $0.014 = $11.77 million.
  4. Coal. 1 kg/kWh → 840,960 tonnes × $6.34 = $5.33 million.
  5. (a) Revenue earned = income − coal = 11.77 − 5.33 = $6.44 million per year (the textbook version of this problem also takes coal cost off the sales income).
  6. (b) Capacity factor = average load ÷ plant capacity = 96/210 = 0.457.

Final answer

  • (a) ≈ $6.44 M per year (sales $11.77 M − coal $5.33 M)
  • (b) capacity factor = 0.457
Wording

If the question says only “annual revenue earned” some markers want the sales income alone ($11.77 M). Write both lines (income and net of coal) and circle the net.

4

Three factors, energy, reserve, outage hours

Textbook solution included

Annual factors: load factor 0.75, capacity factor 0.60, use factor 0.65. Maximum demand 60 MW. Find (a) annual energy, (b) reserve capacity above the peak, (c) hours the plant is out of service per year.

  1. (a) Annual energy. Average load = LF × peak = 0.75×60 = 45 MW → 45 000 kW × 8760 = 394.2×10⁶ kWh.
  2. (b) Reserve capacity. Capacity factor = average/capacity → capacity = 45/0.60 = 75 MW. Reserve = capacity − peak = 75 − 60 = 15 MW.
  3. (c) Hours out of service. Use factor = energy/(capacity × hours in operation) → hours = 394.2×10⁶/(75 000×0.65) = 8 086 h. Out of service = 8760 − 8086 = 674 h.

Final answer

  • (a) 394.2×10⁶ kWh (b) 15 MW (c) 674 h
The three factors on one line

Same energy E in every numerator. Load factor: ÷ (peak × 8760). Capacity factor: ÷ (capacity × 8760). Use factor: ÷ (capacity × hours actually operated). Because the use factor (0.65) is larger than the capacity factor (0.60), the plant must have run fewer than 8760 h.

5

Overall cost per kWh: steam vs hydraulic vs nuclear

Cost comparisonTextbook solution included

Maximum demand 500 MW at 70 % load factor. Compare steam, hydraulic and nuclear: capital cost per MW (Rs 3, 4, 5 crore), interest (6, 5, 5 %), depreciation (6, 4, 5 %), operating cost (30, 5, 15 paise/kWh), transmission and distribution (2, 3, 2 paise/kWh).

Method: cost per kWh = fixed cost per kWh + running cost per kWh.

Energy/yr = LF × peak × 8760 = 0.7 × 500 000 kW × 8760 = 306.6×10⁷ kWh
Fixed cost/yr = (interest% + depreciation%) × capital cost, capital = (Rs crore per MW) × 500 MW
PlantCapitalFixed cost per yearFixed Rs/kWhOperating + T&DOverall
Steam1500 crore180 crore0.5932 paise0.91 Rs/kWh
Hydraulic2000 crore180 crore0.598 paise0.67 Rs/kWh
Nuclear2500 crore250 crore0.8217 paise0.99 Rs/kWh

Final answer

  • Steam 0.91 Rs/kWh · Hydraulic 0.67 Rs/kWh · Nuclear 0.99 Rs/kWh
  • Cheapest overall: hydraulic
Units

1 crore = 10⁷ rupees; 100 paise = 1 rupee. Convert paise to rupees before adding to the fixed cost per kWh.
