Formula sheet (Lecture 10–11)

Range R = Tw1 − Tw2; approach AP = Tw2 − Twb; L/G = water-to-air mass ratio
Energy balance: ha2 = ha1 + (L/G)·cpw(Tw1 − Tw2) (neglecting evaporation loss in the water term)
Merkel number: KaV/L = cpw ∫ dT/(hs − ha) ≈ cpw·R/4 · Σ 1/(hs,i − ha,i)
Four-point Chebyshev: Ti = Tw2 + R·(0.1, 0.4, 0.6, 0.9); ha,i = ha1 + (L/G)cpw(Ti − Tw2)
Moist air: w = 0.622 pv/(p − pv); h = 1.006T + w(2501 + 1.86T) kJ/kg dry air
Evaporation: E = G(w₂ − w₁); make-up = E/L; fan power = Δp·V̇/η
  • Dry tower: the water never touches the air, so the limit is the dry-bulb temperature and sensible heat only (temperature stacking).
  • Wet tower: the limit is the wet-bulb temperature; most of the heat leaves as latent heat of evaporation (≈ 2 % of the water flow).

Counter-flow tower: exit air and Merkel number (Sheet 7 Q1)

Lecture 11No sheet solution

Counter-flow wet tower: water 44 → 29 °C; ambient air 23 °C dry bulb, 20 °C wet bulb. Find the exit-air conditions for 0.1 % evaporation and the tower characteristic for L/G = 1.3.

  1. Inlet air. From Tdb = 23, Twb = 20: w₁ = 0.0134, ha1 = 57.3 kJ/kg.
  2. ha2 = ha1 + 1.3×4.186×(44 − 29) = 139.0 kJ/kg
  3. Exit air. A literal 0.1 % evaporation gives w₂ = w₁ + 0.001×1.3 and T₂ = 99 °C, which is impossible. So the exit air is taken saturated:
    T₂ = 36.45 °C, φ = 100 %, w₂ = 0.0398
    The evaporation is then ≈ 2.03 % of the water flow.
  4. Merkel number (4 points):
    Tw °Chahshs − ha
    30.565.5102.436.9
    35.090.0129.139.1
    38.0106.3150.344.0
    42.5130.8188.357.5
    KaV/L = 4.186×15/4 × Σ 1/(hs − ha) = 1.457

Final answer

  • Exit air ≈ 36.5 °C, saturated
  • KaV/L ≈ 1.46
The 0.1 % figureTaken literally, 0.1 % evaporation does not match an energy balance of this size. State the saturated-exit assumption in the exam and move on.

Cross-flow tower, two wet-bulb cases (Sheet 7 Q2)

Lecture 11No sheet solution

Cross-flow tower, L/G = 1.25, dry bulb 27 °C, range 14 °C, approach 6 °C, 0.5 % evaporation. Find the tower characteristic and exit air for (a) Twb = 24 °C, (b) Twb = 20.5 °C.

(a)

  1. Twb = 24 °C: Tw2 = 30, Tw1 = 44 °C. w₁ = 0.0176; ha1 = 72.1.
  2. ha2 = 72.1 + 1.25×4.186×14 = 145.3; saturated exit ⇒ T₂ = 37.3 °C.
  3. KaV/L = 1.666

(b)

  1. Twb = 20.5 °C: Tw2 = 26.5, Tw1 = 40.5 °C. w₁ = 0.0124; ha1 = 58.9.
  2. ha2 = 58.9 + 1.25×4.186×14 = 132.2; saturated exit ⇒ T₂ = 35.5 °C.
  3. KaV/L = 2.276

Final answer

  • (a) KaV/L ≈ 1.67, exit air 37.3 °C
  • (b) KaV/L ≈ 2.28, exit air 35.5 °C
  • A lower wet bulb needs a bigger tower: Merkel number rises 1.67 → 2.28.
Method noteFor cross-flow the 4-point Merkel is an approximation (the air path changes with height); the counter-flow formula is used and this is stated.

Fill characteristic rewrite (Sheet 7 Q3)

AlgebraShort

Kelly's fill type D, height 3 m, depth 2.67 m: Ka = 0.103 L0.49 G0.51. Rewrite as KaY/L = α(L′/G′)β.

  1. Ka·Y/L = 0.103·Y·L0.49−1·G0.51 = 0.103·Y·L−0.51G0.51 = 0.103·Y·(L/G)−0.51
  2. With Y = 3 m: α = 0.103×3 = 0.309, β = −0.51.

Final answer

  • KaY/L = 0.309 (L′/G′)−0.51
AssumptionL′ and G′ are loadings (kg/s·m²), so the ratio equals L/G; Y is the fill height used in the formula, here 3 m.

Cross-flow tower: air flow and fill (Sheet 7 Q5)

Lecture 11Partly solved

Cross-flow tower cools 4000 kg/min from 40 to 30 °C; ambient 24 °C, 50 % RH; water loading 14 300 kg/h·m²; L/G = 1.0. Estimate the air flow and fill volume.

  1. L = 4000/60 = 66.7 kg/s; G = L/(L/G) = 66.7 kg/s.
  2. Plan area = 4000×60/14 300 = 16.78 m²
  3. w₁ = 0.0093, Twb = 17.1 °C ⇒ AP = 12.9 °C. Merkel number: KaV/L = 0.688.
  4. Fill volume V = (KaV/L)·L/Ka needs Ka for the fill, which the problem does not give; with a Kelly fill constant Ka = α·L′… it follows directly.

Final answer

  • Air flow ≈ 66.7 kg/s
  • Plan area ≈ 16.8 m²; KaV/L ≈ 0.69

Wet tower balance and fan power (Sheet 7 Q7)

Most exam-likeNo sheet solution

A tower receives 7×10⁵ kg/min of water at 35 °C and 5.5×10⁵ kg/min of air at 1 atm, 17 °C, 50 % RH; exit air saturated at 27 °C. Find the exit water temperature, make-up % and fan power (Δp = 85 Pa), induced and forced draft.

  1. L = 11667 kg/s, G = 9167 kg/s. Inlet: w₁ = 0.0060, h₁ = 32.3; exit (27 °C, saturated): w₂ = 0.0227, h₂ = 85.1 kJ/kg.
  2. Evaporation E = G(w₂ − w₁) = 153.0 kg/s
  3. (a) L·cpw(35 − Tw2) = G(h₂ − h₁) − E·cpwTw2 ⇒ Tw2 = 25.43 °C
  4. (b) Make-up = E/L = 1.31 %.
  5. (c) Forced draft: inlet air ρ = 1.212 kg/m³ ⇒ V̇ = 7606 m³/s ⇒ P = 85×7606/1000 = 647 kW. Induced draft: exit ρ = 1.161 ⇒ V̇ = 8078 m³/s ⇒ 687 kW (ideal, η = 1).

Final answer

  • (a) Tw2 ≈ 25.4 °C
  • (b) make-up ≈ 1.31 %
  • (c) 647 kW forced, 687 kW induced
Why induced is largerInduced-draft fans handle the hotter, less dense exit air, so the same Δp needs a bigger volume flow.
