How to get any number from the tables and the Mollier chart

Every steam problem is built from five lookups. Learn these five and you can finish any state point.

A. Saturated, by pressure

Table: p → Tsat, vf, vg, hf, hfg, hg, sf, sfg, sg.
Wet mixture: h = hf + x·hfg, s = sf + x·sfg, v = vf + x·vfg, u = uf + x·ufg.
If the pressure is not listed, interpolate linearly between the two nearest rows.

B. Saturated, by temperature

Use when the temperature is the given: condenser at 40 °C → psat = 0.0738 bar; feed water leaving at 130 °C → h ≈ hf(130 °C) = 546.4. Also used to find the bleed pressure from a saturation temperature.

C. Superheated, p and T

Go to the page for that pressure, find the row for T, read v, h, s. Between two rows: linear interpolation. Between two pressures: interpolate each at the same T, then interpolate between the results.

D. Isentropic expansion (s₂ = s₁)

1) Read s₁. 2) Go to p₂ in the saturation table: compare s₁ with sg. s₁ < sg: wet, x = (s₁ − sf)/sfg, h2s = hf + x·hfg. s₁ > sg: superheated; in the p₂ superheated table find the two rows whose s brackets s₁ and interpolate h.

E. Real expansion

h₂ = h₁ − η(h₁ − h₂ₛ). Then find the real state at (p₂, h₂): if h₂ < hg, x = (h₂ − hf)/hfg; else superheated, interpolate T from h.

F. Pump, compressed liquid

hout,s = hin + vf(pout − pin) with p in kPa (1 bar = 100 kPa). Real pump: divide the rise by ηp. Feed water leaving a heater at T: h ≈ hf at that T.

On the Mollier (h–s) chart

  1. Find a state: intersect two known lines: pressure (curved, labelled in bar) with temperature (in the superheated zone) or with a constant-quality line x = 0.9, 0.95… below the x = 1 curve. Read h on the left axis, s on the bottom axis.
  2. Isentropic expansion: from the start point drop straight down to the exit-pressure line. Read h2s.
  3. Real expansion: compute h₂ = h₁ − η(h₁ − h₂ₛ). On the left axis find h₂, move horizontally until you meet the exit-pressure line. Read x (or T) there, and read s: that s starts the next stage's vertical drop.
  4. Throttling: move horizontally (h constant) to the lower pressure line.
  5. Straight-line expansion (Q4, Q8): draw a ruler line from the inlet state to the exhaust state; each bleed state is where it crosses that bleed pressure's line.
Tolerance

Tables and charts differ by 0.1–1 %. If your answer is within ≈ 2 % of the sheet answer you have the method right; write the table values you used so the marker can follow you.

Minimum superheat for dry exhaust

Trial and errorSolution on sheet

Steam expanding from 10 bar must not contain any moisture at the section where its pressure is 2 bar. What is the minimum degree of superheat needed initially if the isentropic efficiency of the expansion is 90 %?

Idea

“No moisture at 2 bar” means the real exhaust state is saturated vapour: h₂ = hg(2 bar). The unknown is the inlet temperature T₁ that makes this true. You cannot solve for it directly, so you guess T₁ and check (your instructor does exactly this).

η = (h₁ − h₂)/(h₁ − h₂ₛ) with h₂ = hg(2 bar) = 2706.2 and s₂ₛ = s₁
  1. Guess T₁ (any value clearly above Tsat(10 bar) = 179.9 °C). Read h₁ and s₁ from the superheated table at 10 bar.
  2. Isentropic end state. Go down to 2 bar at s = s₁. If s₁ < sg(2 bar) = 7.127 the point is wet: x = (s₁ − sf)/sfg, h₂ₛ = hf + x·hfg. If s₁ > sg it is superheated: interpolate h in the 2 bar superheated table at s = s₁. Mollier: start at (10 bar, T₁), drop vertically to the 2 bar line, read h₂ₛ.
  3. Actual end state h₂ = h₁ − 0.9(h₁ − h₂ₛ). Compare with hg(2 bar). h₂ too low → wet → raise T₁. Too high → lower T₁.

Four-stage turbine: condition line, reheat factor

Mollier practiceSolution on sheet

In a four-stage turbine the steam expands from 16.3 bar, 270 °C to 0.068 bar. Intermediate stage pressures are 6.1, 1.6 and 0.4 bar; the stage efficiency is 73 %. Draw the condition line and determine (a) the reheat factor, (b) the condition of the steam at the end of each stage, (c) the internal efficiency of the turbine, (d) the power in kW if ηmech = 94 % and ηgen = 96 %.

Method: repeat one stage four times

hout = hin − ηstage(hin − hout,s) hout,s = h(pout, s = sin)

Each stage starts from the real end state of the stage before. Its isentropic reference is a vertical drop (constant entropy) from that state, not from state 1.

  1. State 1. 16.3 bar, 270 °C (superheated table, interpolate pressure between 15 and 20 bar): h₁ = 2966.1, s₁ = 6.7539.
  2. Each stage. Mollier: from the stage inlet go straight down to the next pressure line → hout,s. Compute hout. Then locate (hout, pout) on the chart and read s (the start of the next vertical drop) and the quality x.
    Stagep out (bar)h out,sh outcondition at exit
    1→26.12757.02813.5superheated
    2→31.62572.22637.4wet, x = 0.974
    3→40.42419.92478.6wet, x = 0.932
    4→50.0682238.32303.2wet, x = 0.889
  3. Isentropic drop for the whole turbine (state 1 to 0.068 bar at s = s₁): h5,ss = 2093.7. Single-stage ideal work = h₁ − h5,ss = 872.4 kJ/kg.
  4. (a) Reheat factor = sum of stage isentropic drops ÷ overall isentropic drop:
    RF = Σ(hin − hout,s)/(h₁ − h₅,ss) = 908.2/872.4 = 1.041
  5. (c) Turbine internal efficiency = actual drop ÷ ideal drop:
    ηT = (h₁ − h₅)/(h₁ − h₅,ss) = 663.0/872.4 = 0.760
    Cross-check: ηT = RF·ηstage = 1.041×0.73 = 0.760 ✓
  6. (d) Power per kg/s of steam.
    P = (h₁ − h₅)·ηm·ηg = 663.0×0.94×0.96 = 598 kW per kg/s

Final answer

  • (a) RF = 1.041 (sheet: 1.041)
  • (b) Stage ends: superheated, x = 0.97, x = 0.93, x = 0.89
  • (c) ηT = 0.760 (sheet: 0.76)
  • (d) ≈ 598 kW per kg/s of steam (sheet: 598)
Always check

Every exit enthalpy must be greater than its isentropic one (hout > hout,s); otherwise you have a sign slip. RF must come out between 1.04 and 1.08. (Your sheet shows x = 0.96 for the last stage; the chart-free value is x ≈ 0.89, so don't worry if your reading wobbles in the second digit.)

Three feed-water heaters: choose bleeds, find extractions

Straight-line expansionSolution on sheet

Steam is supplied to a turbine at 27 bar, 350 °C and exhausted to the condenser at 0.07 bar with dryness 0.88. The expansion line is a straight line on the h–s chart. Three feed-water heaters raise the feed water from 40 °C to 130 °C. Drains are cascaded between heaters back to the condenser. Select bleeding points and compute the amount extracted at each point per kg of feed water.

Step 1: choose the bleeding points

The 90 °C rise is split equally: 3 heaters → 30 °C each, so the feed water leaves heaters at 70, 100, 130 °C. Assume a terminal temperature difference of 5 °C, so the bled steam's saturation temperature is 5 °C above each outlet: 75, 105, 135 °C → p = 0.386, 1.209, 3.132 bar (saturation table, entered by temperature).

Step 2: read the bleed enthalpies on the straight line

Solve in order

Start at the heater with the highest pressure: it only has one unknown (its own bleed). Each lower heater adds one new unknown plus the drain already found. Never solve all three at once.

Willans lineSolution on sheet

A 300 MW turbine is governed by throttling. At full load: 55 bar, 320 °C, 360 kg/s. No-load steam is 12 % of full-load steam. Exhaust pressure 0.076 bar; internal efficiency 77 % constant. Find steam consumption, nozzle-box (chest) pressure and exhaust condition at 0, 25, 40, 70, 85 and 100 % load. Also find the power spent overcoming losses at no load.

A turbine with simple throttling: no-load steam = 12 % of full load. Nozzle-box at full load 18 bar, 290 °C. Exhaust 0.05 bar. Full-load specific steam consumption 4.55 kg/kWh. Find the cycle and turbine internal efficiencies at 20, 40, 60, 80 % loads. Sketch the Willans line and the pressure variation.

Idea

No efficiency is given, so get the work per kg at full load from the steam rate, then scale it with the load. Power = ṁ·Δh, so for the same fraction of full-load power the work per kg changes with the flow fraction:

SSC = 3600/Δh → Δh_FL = 3600/4.55 = 791.2 kJ/kg
P/P_FL = (ṁ/ṁ_FL)·(Δh/Δh_FL) → Δh = (L/(ṁ/ṁ_FL))·Δh_FL
η_cycle = Δh/(h₁ − hf(0.05 bar)) η_T = Δh/(h₁ − h₂ₛ) h₁ = 3007.0 (18 bar, 290 °C) is constant; hf = 137.8

Each row: ṁ/ṁ_FL = 0.12 + 0.88 L; p_chest = 18·(ṁ/ṁ_FL); then h₂ₛ = h at (0.05 bar, s of the chest state).
