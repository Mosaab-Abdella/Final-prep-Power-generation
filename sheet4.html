Formula sheet for diesel / IC-engine problems (your instructor's notation)

Indicated power IP = n · pmi · L · A · N · K · 10/6 [kW] (p in bar, L in m, A in m², N in rpm)

n = number of cylinders · K = ½ for a four-stroke, 1 for a two-stroke (one power stroke per revolution) · for a double-acting engine, add the cover-side and crank-side IP separately. Where 10/6 comes from: p[bar]×10⁵ Pa × L·A [m³] × (N·K/60) power strokes per second ÷ 1000 W/kW = p·L·A·N·K × 10⁵/60 000 = p·L·A·N·K × 10/6.

Brake power BP = 2πNT/(60 000) = W·N/Cd (with a dynamometer constant) or BP = π(W − S)(Db + d)N/(60 000) (rope brake)
pmi = (indicator-diagram area ÷ length) × spring constant [mm² ÷ mm × bar/mm = bar]
ηmech = BP/IP; FP = IP − BP; bmep = pmi·ηmech (same engine, so the ratio is the same)
bsfc = ṁf/BP [kg/kWh]; ηth,B = BP/(ṁf·CV); ηth,I = IP/(ṁf·CV); ηrelative = ηth,I/ηair-standard
Diesel cycle: η = 1 − (1/(γ rγ−1))·(ργ − 1)/(ρ − 1), ρ = V₃/V₂ (cut-off ratio); Otto: η = 1 − 1/rγ−1
ηvol = volume of air actually drawn per minute ÷ swept volume per minute; swept volume/min = n·A·L·N·K
Air through the orifice: Va = 840 · Ao · Cd · √(hw/ρa) [m³/min], hw in cm of water, ρa = p/(RT)
Theoretical air per kg fuel = [ (8/3)·C + 8·H ]/0.23 (C, H = mass fractions; air is 23 % O₂ by mass)
Air/fuel from exhaust analysis: mair/mfuel = N₂% · C%/(33 (CO₂% + CO%))

Heat balance sheet (always on a per-minute basis unless told otherwise)

Heat supplied = ṁf · CV; heat to BP = BP × 60; heat to cooling water = ṁw·cp,w·ΔT; heat to exhaust = ṁg·cp,g·(Texh − Troom) with ṁg = ṁair + ṁfuel; unaccounted = supplied − the three

When the fuel contains hydrogen, 9 kg of steam is formed per kg of H₂. Treat the steam separately: its cp is given (2.09 kJ/kg·K) and the remaining gas is the “dry” exhaust.

Double-acting four-stroke diesel: mechanical efficiency

Sheet solution available

Four-stroke double-acting diesel: D = 200 mm, stroke 350 mm, mean effective pressures 6.5 bar (cover side) and 7 bar (crank side), 420 rpm, piston rod 20 mm. Rope brake: dead load 1370 N, spring balance 145 N, drum 1.2 m, rope 20 mm. Find the mechanical efficiency.

  1. Areas. Cover side A = π/4·0.2² = 0.03142 m². Crank side loses the rod: A = π/4(0.2² − 0.02²) = 0.03110 m².
  2. IP, each side (K = ½, n = 1):
    IPcover = 6.5×0.35×0.03142×420×½×10/6 = 25.01 kW IPcrank = 7×0.35×0.03110×420×½×10/6 = 26.67 kW
    Total IP = 51.68 kW.
  3. BP from the rope brake (effective diameter 1.2 + 0.02 = 1.22 m):
    BP = π(1370 − 145)(1.22)(420)/60 000 = 32.87 kW
  4. ηmech = BP/IP = 32.87/51.68 = 63.59 %

Final answer

  • IP = 51.68 kW, BP = 32.87 kW, ηmech = 63.6 %
Matches your notebookYour instructor's solution gives IP = 25 + 26.67 = 51.67 kW, BP = 32.86 kW and 63.59 %.

Otto engine: bore and stroke from power

Sheet solution available

Four-stroke Otto engine: BP = 14.7 kW, suction pressure 0.9 bar, ηmech = 80 %, r = 5, compression index 1.35, expansion index 1.3, maximum pressure 24 bar, 1000 rpm, stroke : bore = 1.5. Find bore and stroke.

  1. Pressures.
    p₂ = p₁ r1.35 = 0.9×51.35 = 7.90 bar; p₄ = p₃ (1/r)1.3 = 24×(1/5)1.3 = 2.96 bar
  2. Work per cycle (V₂ = V₃ = 1 unit, V₁ = V₄ = 5 units):
    W = (p₃V₃ − p₄V₄)/(1.3 − 1) − (p₂V₂ − p₁V₁)/(1.35 − 1) = 20.91·10⁵ N·m per unit of V₃
  3. Mean effective pressure = W/(V₄ − V₃) = 20.91/4 = 5.23 bar.
  4. IP = BP/ηmech = 14.7/0.8 = 18.37 kW.
  5. Size. With L = 1.5 D, A = πD²/4, K = ½, n = 1:
    18.37 = 5.23 × 1.5D × (π/4)D² × 1000 × ½ × 10/6 ⇒ D³ = 0.00358 m³
    D = 153 mm, L = 1.5D = 229 mm

Final answer

  • D ≈ 153 mm, L ≈ 229 mm (pm = 5.23 bar)
Notebook arithmeticYour instructor's notebook gets pm = 5.245 bar (same as mine to rounding) but then D = 0.1418 m, L = 0.213 m. Substituting pm = 5.245 into IP = pm·1.5D·(π/4)D²·(1000/2)·10/6 gives D³ = 0.00357 m³, so D ≈ 0.153 m. I trust 0.153 m; check the cube root yourself.

Two-stroke C.I. engine with cut-off

Sheet solution available

Two-cylinder two-stroke C.I. engine, r = 13, 200 mm × 250 mm, 14 kg/h fuel at 300 rpm, relative efficiency 65 %, mechanical efficiency 76 %, fuel injected for 5 % of the stroke, CV 41 800 kJ/kg. Find the mean effective pressure.

  1. Cut-off ratio. V₃ − V₂ = 0.05 Vs = 0.05 (V₁ − V₂) = 0.05 (13V₂ − V₂) = 0.6 V₂ ⇒ V₃ = 1.6 V₂, ρ = 1.6.
  2. Air-standard efficiency (γ = 1.4):
    η = 1 − [1/(1.4×130.4)]·(1.61.4 − 1)/(1.6 − 1) = 0.6028
  3. Indicated thermal efficiency = ηrel × ηair-std = 0.65×0.6028 = 0.3918.
  4. IP = ηth,I·ṁf·CV = 0.3918×162.56 kW = 63.7 kW; BP = 0.76 IP = 48.4 kW.
  5. m.e.p. (n = 2, K = 1): IP = 2·p·0.25·(π/4)0.2²·300·10/6
    pmi = 8.11 bar; pmb = 6.16 bar

Final answer

  • pmi ≈ 8.11 bar, pmb ≈ 6.16 bar
Small difference from the notebookThe notebook uses ηair-std = 0.615 (so ηth = 0.40, IP = 65 kW, 8.27 / 6.28 bar). Evaluating the formula exactly gives 0.603, which leads to the values above. If your instructor's value is the one marked, quote his ηair-std and carry on.

6-cylinder diesel test (the repeated exam question)

Exam hit: Finals 2024, 2025, 2026No sheet solution: derived here

The question that appears in the Fall 2024, 2025 and 2026 finals. 6-cylinder four-stroke diesel: bore 125 mm, stroke 125 mm, 2400 rpm, dynamometer load 490 N, dynamometer constant 16 100; air orifice 55 mm, Cd = 0.66, head 310 mm of water; barometer 760 mm Hg, ambient 25 °C; fuel 22.1 kg/h, CV 45 100 kJ/kg; fuel 85 % C, 15 % H; suction-stroke air at 1.013 bar, 25 °C. Calculate: (i) bmep, (ii) specific fuel consumption, (iii) brake thermal efficiency, (iv) volumetric efficiency, (v) percentage of excess air.

  1. Brake power.
    BP = W·N/Cd = 490×2400/16 100 = 73.04 kW
  2. (i) bmep. A = (π/4)(0.125)² = 0.012272 m²; n = 6, K = ½:
    pmb = BP·6/(n·L·A·N·K·10) = 73.04×6/(6×0.125×0.012272×2400×½×10) = 3.97 bar
  3. (ii) Specific fuel consumption = ṁf/BP = 22.1/73.04 = 0.3026 kg/kWh.
  4. (iii) Brake thermal efficiency
    ηB = BP/(ṁf·CV) = 73.04/((22.1/3600)×45 100) = 26.4 %
  5. (iv) Volumetric efficiency. Air density at suction conditions: ρa = p/(RT) = 1.013×10⁵/(287×298.15) = 1.184 kg/m³. Orifice area Ao = (π/4)(0.055)² = 0.002376 m². With the head in cm of water (31 cm):
    Va = 840·Ao·Cd·√(hw/ρa) = 840×0.002376×0.66×√(31/1.184) = 6.74 m³/min
    Swept volume per minute = n·A·L·N·K = 6×0.012272×0.125×2400×½ = 11.04 m³/min.
    ηvol = 6.74/11.04 = 61.0 %
  6. (v) Excess air. Mass of air = ρaVa = 7.98 kg/min; fuel = 22.1/60 = 0.3683 kg/min ⇒ actual A/F = 21.66.
    Theoretical O₂ = (8/3)(0.85) + 8(0.15) = 3.467 kg/kg fuel ⇒ theoretical air = 3.467/0.23 = 15.07 kg/kg.
    Excess air = (21.66 − 15.07)/15.07 = 43.7 %

Final answer

  • (i) bmep ≈ 3.97 bar
  • (ii) sfc ≈ 0.303 kg/kWh
  • (iii) ηB ≈ 26.4 %
  • (iv) ηvol ≈ 61.0 %
  • (v) excess air ≈ 44 %
Units trap in the 840 formulaThe constant 840 only works with the head in cm of water. With 310 mm you must enter 31. I checked it from first principles: Δp = ρwg·h = 1000×9.81×0.31 = 3 041 Pa, V = CdAo√(2Δp/ρa)×60 = 6.74 m³/min, the same as 6.74. Writing the first-principles line in your answer is the safest way to get the marks.
Exam routine for this questionWrite the six lines in this order: BP, bmep, sfc, ηB, Va and ηvol, air/fuel and excess air. It is almost pure substitution, so a two-minute check of units pays off. Note the 15 % H and 85 % C feed only the theoretical-air step.

Supercharging with a mechanically driven blower

Exam-styleNo sheet solution

A C.I. engine develops 13 kW indicated power per m³/min of free air induced. Engine: 3 litres, four-stroke, 3500 rpm, ηvol = 0.81 (free air 1.013 bar, 15 °C). A mechanically driven blower (ηs = 0.72, pressure ratio 1.72) is added; the cylinders then hold a charge equal to the swept volume at the blower delivery state. Find the increase in brake power.

  1. Naturally aspirated. Free air = 0.81×0.003×3500/2 = 4.253 m³/min ⇒ IP = 13×4.253 = 55.3 kW.
  2. Blower delivery temperature (γ = 1.4): T₂ = T₁[1 + (r0.2857 − 1)/ηs] = 288.15[1 + (1.720.2857 − 1)/0.72] = 355.2 K; p₂ = 1.72×1.013 = 1.742 bar.
  3. Charge per minute = swept volume × N/2 = 5.25 m³/min at (p₂, T₂). Converted to free-air volume: V = 5.25×(p₂/p₁)(T₁/T₂) = 7.32 m³/min.
  4. IPs = 13×7.32 = 95.2 kW; ΔIP = 39.9 kW
  5. Blower power = ṁcp(T₂ − T₁) with ṁ = p₁V/(RT₁) = 0.150 kg/s: 10.1 kW.
    Increase in brake power = ΔIP − blower power = 39.9 − 10.1 = 29.9 kW

Final answer

  • ΔBP ≈ 30 kW (IP rises from 55.3 to 95.2 kW)
AssumptionI assumed the friction power is unchanged, so the gain in brake power is the gain in indicated power minus the blower power. The question does not state the blower's mechanical efficiency.

Fuel saving from reduced friction

Exam-style

An engine must develop 100 kW with ηmech = 86 % and uses 55 kg/h of fuel. A design improvement lowers friction by 4.8 kW; indicated thermal efficiency stays the same. Find the fuel saving.

  1. IP = 100/0.86 = 116.28 kW ⇒ FP = 16.28 kW. New FP = 16.28 − 4.8 = 11.48 kW.

  3. Same ηth,I ⇒ fuel ∝ IP:
    ṁ′ = 55×111.48/116.28 = 52.73 kg/h; saving = 2.27 kg/h

Final answer

  • Saving ≈ 2.27 kg/h
